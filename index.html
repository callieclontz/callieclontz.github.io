Research

My research focuses on the formation history of the Milky Way's largest and most enigmatic star cluster, Omega Centauri. This complex star cluster stands out against classical globular clusters in many ways and is thought to be the stripped nuclear star cluster of one of our galaxy's most recent and impactful mergers. Understanding its seeding and assembly grants us insights into how galactic centers take shape and gets us closer to unraveling the mystery of the multiple stellar population phenomenon only observed in dense star clusters.
My PhD worked utilized two well-studied probes of nuclear star cluster formation, the age-metallicity relation (AMR) and the Chromosome Diagram (ChD), to study Omega Centauri.

Precise Ages of Individual Stars

  • In Clontz+ 2024, I constrained precise ages for more than 8,000 individual subgiant branch (SGB) stars using updated isochrone models.
  • In the age-metallicity relation (AMR), I discovered two distinct sequences.
  • The tighter, supposedly self-enriching sequence is more metal-poor at a fixed age than the seemingly accreted component, which is inconsistent with current models of nuclear star cluster (NSC) formation.
  • The lack of stars with both age and abundance information makes interpretation difficult.
  • To address this gap, I successfully proposed high-resolution spectroscopic observations using FLAMES@VLT for approximately 120 stars occupying the two streams.
  • I executed these observations in visitor mode in April 2025 and the analysis of these data is ongoing (see below).

Helium Enhancements as a Function of Metallicity

  • The ages study focused on the SGB to avoid model complications due to unknown helium abundances, and spectroscopic helium constraints for individual stars are observationally challenging to obtain.
  • Therefore, in my second PhD work (Clontz+ 2025), I focused on the helium enhancement between the P1 (primoridial) population and the P2 (hot-hydrogen-burning polluted) populations on the red-giant branch (RGB) in Omega Centauri to better constrain its enhancement as a function of metallicity.
  • I found that the P2 population exhibits a rapid increase in helium enhancement until [Fe/H] ~ -1.9, after which the relationship is flat at Delta Y = 0.154.
  • This signals a change in the efficiency of helium production and a major shift in the mode of stellar evolution of the P2 population.
  • Delta Y = 0.15 indicates a total helium mass fraction of Y = 0.40 for the P2 stars which is unprecedented in our Milky Way.
  • The polluter source for such high helium abundances is still unknown and is actively under investigation.
  • This open question has important implication for star formation and evolution in ultra-high stellar density environments and at high redshift.

Individual Stellar Population Parsing

  • My most recent work (Clontz+ 2026) finally showed the connection between the two assembly probes, the AMR and the ChD, to connect the P1, Im, and P2 populations with the two star formation tracks of the AMR.
  • By combining statistical and machine learning methods I arrived at subpopulation labels for over 53k stars across our 14 distinct subpopulations, covering 12.8 $<$ F814W $<$ 19.3.
  • I then matched these with the catalog from my 2024 work, assigning ages to $>$2700 stars.
  • I (and the community) can now see, for the first time, how the P1, Im, and P2 populations of the ChD map onto the two-stream AMR.

Abundances of SGB Stars: Connecting AMR with Chemistry

  • My current work is analyzing the spectra of 120 sub-giant branch stars to combine chemical abundances and ages for stars in $\omega$ Cen for the first time.
  • This will allow us to assert the chemical enrichment environments of the two streams of the age-metallicity relation.
  • I proposed, planned, and executed these observations in visitor mode and have also performed all of the data reduction steps myself.
  • I have stacked the 6 exposures I have for each star and am now working on measuring stellar parameters and detailed chemical abundances of around a dozen elements using TSFitPy.
